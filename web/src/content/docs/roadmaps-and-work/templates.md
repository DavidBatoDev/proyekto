A template is a published roadmap structure you can start from instead of a
blank canvas — a website build, an app launch, a rebrand, whatever someone has
done enough times to write down. Applying one gives you a real roadmap you own
outright, not a link to someone else's.

## The gallery

Browse templates by **category**, narrow by **tag**, and sort by what other
people have found useful. Each entry shows its **rating** — the average left by
people who have actually applied it — alongside who published it.

Open one and you get a **preview** before you commit to anything: the epics it
contains, the features under them, and the shape of the tree you would end up
with. Read the preview properly. A template with forty features is a different
proposition from one with eight, and the preview is where you find that out
rather than after it lands in your roadmap.

## Versions, and why your copy is independent

A template can be **republished** by its author as it improves. You always apply
a **specific version**.

> The moment you apply a template, your roadmap is an independent copy. It does
> not track the template, and a later version published by the author will
> never reach back and change work you have already started.

That is the behaviour you want. A roadmap that quietly restructured itself
because someone else edited a template would make the plan unquotable. If a
newer version has something you want, apply it to a new roadmap and take what is
useful across by hand.

## Applying to a new roadmap, or merging into an existing one

| | Apply to a new roadmap | Merge into an existing roadmap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Result** | A fresh roadmap that *is* the template's structure | The template's epics and features added alongside what you already have |
| **Use when** | Starting a piece of work from a known shape | You have a plan going and want a proven block added to it |
| **Watch for** | Nothing — the canvas was empty | Duplicate-sounding epics; prune after merging |

After a merge, spend five minutes on the tree. A template does not know what you
have already planned, so two similar epics is a normal outcome and an easy fix —
move the features you want into one epic and delete the empty one. Every one of
those moves is recorded in
[change history](/docs/roadmaps-and-work/change-history), so it is safe to be
decisive.

## Rating a template

Once you have used one, rate it. The rating is what makes the next person's
choice a real choice rather than a guess, and it is the only signal in the
gallery that comes from use rather than from the author.

Rate the *structure*, not the project — a template that was wrong for your work
but well built is not a bad template.

## Publishing your own

Publishing a template is available to **verified consultants**, and it is a
**web-only** part of Proyekto — the mobile app does not include it.

Becoming a consultant means applying and being approved by Proyekto; once
verified, publishing templates sits alongside the other things that unlock. See
[Selling on Proyekto](/docs/clients-and-marketplace/selling) for what that
involves.

If you are not a consultant, you can still reuse structure: duplicate a roadmap
you built, or keep one project as the pattern you copy from.

## Plans and limits

**Templates are on every plan.** Browsing the gallery, applying a template and
rating one are not gated. See [Plans](/docs/workspaces-and-plans/plans) for what
each tier includes.

What still applies is the **node limit**. Every epic, feature and task a template
creates is a node, and plans cap nodes per roadmap. A large template applied to
a roadmap that is already near its limit will stop partway rather than exceed
it, and Proyekto tells you which limit you reached.

> Reaching the limit only blocks new nodes. Everything already in your roadmap —
> including whatever the template did add — stays exactly as it is, readable and
> editable. Nothing is removed, hidden or rolled back to make room.

If that happens, the usual options are to prune the tree, apply the template to
a new roadmap instead, or look at
[Limits and usage](/docs/workspaces-and-plans/limits-and-usage) to see where you
stand.
