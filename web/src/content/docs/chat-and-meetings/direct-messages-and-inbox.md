A direct message is a one-to-one conversation with someone you work with, and
the Inbox is where all of them — plus the project conversations you are in —
land in one list. They are two halves of the same answer to "where do I look
when I do not know which project it was in?".

## Starting a direct message

You can DM anyone who shares a project with you. That is the whole rule: if you
both have access to at least one project, you can start a conversation; if you
do not, there is nobody to pick.

DMs are still project-scoped underneath. A conversation belongs to the project
you share, which is what keeps the boundary honest — a message about a client's
work sits inside that client's project rather than floating free in a personal
inbox that outlives your access to it. If you work with the same person on two
projects, you will have a conversation in each.

> Losing access to a project takes its conversations with it. That is the point:
> chat is part of the work, not a separate address book.

## The Inbox

The Inbox is your cross-project surface, and it does not care which project you
happen to be looking at. It lists:

- **Your direct messages**, from everywhere, in their own group.
- **The project conversations you are in**, grouped by project.

Groups collapse, so a project you are ignoring this week can be folded away.
Everything is ordered by recency, with unread conversations marked, and
selecting one opens it right there — you do not have to navigate into the
project first to reply.

The composer in the Inbox is the same composer as in a channel, so attachments,
replies, reactions, editing and unsending all behave exactly as described in
[Project chat](/docs/chat-and-meetings/project-chat). Unsending still leaves the
*This message was deleted* placeholder; editing still marks the message
**(edited)**.

## How a mention reaches you

Being `@`-mentioned is the one thing in chat designed to interrupt you, and it
takes up to four routes:

| Route | When |
| --- | --- |
| **The Inbox** | Always — the conversation appears with an unread marker |
| **Your notification list** | Always |
| **Push** | Only in the installed iOS or Android app, if you allowed it |
| **Email** | If your email settings still have mentions switched on |

All of it is tunable in one place, and it is an account-level setting rather
than a per-workspace one — see
[Notifications](/docs/account-and-apps/notifications). A new direct message
notifies the same way, including the first message from someone you have never
spoken to.

## Marking things read

Opening a conversation marks it read up to its newest message. That is what
clears its marker in the Inbox and, once nothing is left unread, the badge on
the Inbox itself. Nothing clears because a notification was delivered or an
email was opened — only actually opening the conversation counts, which is why
the badge is a reliable "I have not looked at this" rather than "this was sent".

If a conversation keeps reappearing, someone is still posting in it. Leaving a
channel removes it from your list entirely; for a DM, there is nothing to leave
— it simply falls down the list as it goes quiet.

## What the Inbox does not cover

The Inbox is conversations. It is not an activity feed, and two common things
deliberately do not appear in it:

- **Task assignments** — being added as an assignee notifies you, but the task
  is in the roadmap, not in a conversation. The full set assigned to you across
  every project is the [Command
  center](/docs/roadmaps-and-work/command-center).
- **Register events** — a change request submitted or decided, a deliverable
  sent for review, a decision finalised. Those are records, and they live in
  their registers; see [Delivery and
  governance](/docs/delivery-governance/overview-governance).

Both of those reach you through
[Notifications](/docs/account-and-apps/notifications) instead. Keeping them out
of the Inbox is what stops it becoming a feed you stop reading: if something is
in your Inbox, a person is waiting for a reply.
