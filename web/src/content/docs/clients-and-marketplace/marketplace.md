The marketplace is where Proyekto connects the people who need work done with
the consultants and talent who can do it. It lives on the web only — the
installed Proyekto app on iOS and Android deliberately leaves it out, so if you
are reading this on your phone, open proyekto.tech in a browser to follow along.

## Web only, and why

The mobile app is built for delivery: your projects, roadmaps, tasks, chat and
meetings. The marketplace, seller profiles and everything financial stay on the
web. Nothing is missing from your account — the same login opens both — the app
simply does not carry those pages. See [The mobile
app](/docs/account-and-apps/mobile-app).

## Browsing the category tree

Consultants and talent are organised in a three-level tree:

**Category → Subcategory → Topic**

A category is the broad field. A subcategory narrows it to a discipline, and a
topic narrows it again to the specific thing you want done. Each level is its own
page with its own web address, so a topic page is a link you can send to a
colleague and it will open on exactly the same shortlist you were looking at.

Start wide when you are not sure what you need and let the tree teach you the
vocabulary; start at a topic when you already know. Along the way you can also
browse consultants and talent directly, and filter what comes back.

> The tree describes *what people do*. It says nothing about availability — that
> is what a profile and a conversation are for.

## Reading a consultant profile

A profile is the seller's own page, written by them. Expect their headline and
description of what they do, the specialities they work in, links to their work,
and the services they have published. Talent profiles work the same way and
appear in the same tree.

The one part a seller does not write themselves is the **verified** mark. A
consultant becomes verified by applying to Proyekto and being approved by a
reviewer — it is a check made by the platform, not a claim made by the person.
Only verified consultants appear in the public directory, and only they can
discover talent, publish roadmap templates and use the finance surfaces.

Verified means the application was reviewed and approved. It is not a guarantee
of outcome, a rating, or a promise about any particular piece of work. Read the
profile, look at the linked work, and talk to them.

## Posting a project brief

If you would rather describe the work and let people come to you, post a brief.

A brief is a written description of work to be done. The useful ones say:

| Write this | Not this |
| --- | --- |
| The outcome you want | A list of technologies |
| What "done" looks like | "Ongoing" |
| Constraints that actually bind — deadline, regulated domain, existing stack | Everything you can think of |
| Roughly how big it is | Nothing at all |

Pick the category the work belongs to — that is how the right people find it —
and set a timeline. Posted briefs are visible to sellers browsing the marketplace,
who filter them by category and timeline. Responses reach you in Proyekto, through
your notifications and the marketplace pages, so there is no separate inbox to
watch.

You can post more than one brief, and a brief is not a commitment. Nothing about
posting one starts work or creates an obligation.

## From a brief to a project

A brief is a description. A **project** is where delivery actually happens, and
moving from one to the other is a deliberate step you take once you have agreed
with someone.

Create the project, build or generate its roadmap, and grant the consultant
access to it. Remember the rule from [Core
concepts](/docs/start-here/core-concepts): a workspace seat grants nothing inside
a project, and project access is a separate, per-person grant on the ladder
owner → admin → editor → commenter → viewer. An outside consultant can deliver on
your project without joining your workspace at all. See [Creating a
project](/docs/projects/creating-a-project) and [Project access and
roles](/docs/projects/access-and-roles).

If the arrangement is going to be a paid one, the agreement itself is a contract,
and the money side is invoices — both covered in [Contracts and
invoices](/docs/clients-and-marketplace/contracts-and-invoices).

## Services are not briefs

The two look similar in a list and work in opposite directions:

- A **service** is authored by the seller. It is a defined offering they have
  published and are ready to deliver — you browse them and pick one.
- A **brief** is authored by you. It describes work that does not exist yet and
  invites people to respond.

Use a service when someone already sells the thing you want. Write a brief when
your work is specific enough that nobody has listed it.
