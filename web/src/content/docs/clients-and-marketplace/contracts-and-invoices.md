Contracts record what was agreed; invoices record what was billed and what has
been settled. Both live on the web only — the installed Proyekto app leaves the
marketplace and finance pages out, so open a browser for anything on this page.
The single most important thing to understand comes first.

## Proyekto does not handle money

> Payment happens outside Proyekto. Proyekto records that an invoice was paid. It
> never takes, holds or transfers money.

Whatever you and your client agree on — bank transfer, a card, a payment
processor, a cheque — happens between you, by your own means. What you do here is
keep an accurate record of it. Every status below describes a fact you are
recording, not a movement of funds.

## Creating a contract

A contract starts as a **draft**, attached to the working relationship it covers.
You fill in who the parties are and the commercial terms — the charging model and
the terms that will drive every invoice raised against it.

Two rate worlds are kept apart deliberately: the rates on a contract are what the
**client** is charged, while what your own team members are paid stays internal —
see [Rates and currency](/docs/teams-time-and-rates/rates-and-currency).

A draft is yours to edit freely, and to delete if the deal does not happen.

## The two signing paths

When the draft is ready, it goes for signature. There are two routes, and the
second one is the reason clients do not need to be talked into creating an
account:

1. **Signed in-app.** The other party has a Proyekto account. They open the
   contract in Proyekto, read it, and sign it there.
2. **Signed by link.** You send a tokenized link. They open it in any browser
   with no account, no login and nothing to install, read the document and sign.

Both produce the same signed contract. The link is single-purpose: it opens that
one agreement and nothing else in Proyekto, and an expired or revoked link tells
the visitor it is no longer active rather than showing the document.

Once signed, the terms lock. Editing resumes only through an amendment.

## Amendments

Work changes. An **amendment** is how an already-signed contract changes with it,
without pretending the original never happened.

From the signed contract, choose to amend the terms. You then edit an amendment
draft rather than the signed document — and nothing is saved while you do it. Two
ways out:

- **Discard** — the draft is thrown away and you are back at exactly what was
  signed. Nothing was touched.
- **Apply the change** — you choose which invoices the new terms apply to, and
  the amendment takes effect.

Both sides see the contract as amended, with the agreed terms that now apply.
History is kept: an amendment is a new layer on the record, not a rewrite of it.

## The invoice lifecycle

An invoice moves through a fixed set of states. In order:

| State | What it means | How it gets there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Being prepared. Not visible to the client, fully editable. | You create it |
| **Issue and send** | The action that commits it and emails it to the client. | You choose it |
| **Issued** | Sent, awaiting payment. | Automatically, on issuing |
| **Overdue** | Issued, still outstanding, past its due date. | Automatically, by date |
| **Partially paid** | Some of the balance has been recorded as received. | You record a payment |
| **Paid** | Nothing outstanding. | You record payment covering the balance |

**Overdue** is not a separate thing you set. It is what an issued invoice becomes
when its due date passes with a balance still outstanding, and it stops being
overdue the moment the balance is settled.

### Recording a payment

When money arrives — outside Proyekto, by whatever means you agreed — you record
it against the invoice.

- Record less than the outstanding balance and the invoice becomes **partially
  paid**, with the remainder still outstanding and still tracked.
- Record enough to cover the balance and it becomes **paid**, with nothing
  outstanding.
- Recorded something wrong? A payment can be reversed, which puts the invoice
  back to the state its remaining balance implies.

An issued invoice that should not have gone out can be **voided**, which creates
a replacement draft to correct — issued invoices are not edited in place, because
the client already has the original.

## Where they live and who sees them

Contracts and invoices appear in two places, and it is the same data in both:

- **Inside a project**, on its contract and invoices pages, alongside the work
  being delivered.
- **In your finance portfolio** in the marketplace, which gathers every contract
  and invoice across the relationships you are part of and flags what is overdue.

Both are gated. The finance surfaces are part of what being a verified consultant
unlocks — see [Selling on Proyekto](/docs/clients-and-marketplace/selling) — and
inside a project, what you can see follows project access, so someone with a
viewer or commenter grant is not being shown your commercial terms. A person
signing by link sees the one document that link opens, and nothing more.
