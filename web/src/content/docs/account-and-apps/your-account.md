Your Proyekto account is one identity that travels with you. The same sign-in
reaches every workspace you belong to, every project you have been given access
to, and the mobile app. This page covers how you get in, what other people see
of you, and which settings follow you around versus which belong to a workspace.

## Signing in

There are two ways into an account, and both are available on every plan:

- **Email and password** — sign up with an email address, choose a password.
- **Google** — one click, no password to keep. Available on every plan, not
  just the paid ones.

They are the same account. If you signed up with email and later use Google
with that same address, you land in the account you already had.

### Verifying your email

A new email-and-password account sends a verification link to the address you
gave. Click it and you are done. Until you do, expect Proyekto to keep nudging
you — an unverified address is one that cannot receive an invite, a mention
email or a password reset, which makes it a dead end rather than an account.

### Forgotten password

**Forgot password** on the sign-in screen sends a reset link. If nothing
arrives, check that you are asking for the address you actually signed up with,
and look in spam. If you signed up with Google there is no password to reset —
use the Google button instead.

## Your profile

Your profile is the version of you that other people in Proyekto see: your
name, avatar and the details you choose to fill in. It is what renders beside
your chat messages, on task assignments, in a project's Team list and on
comments you leave.

Edit it from **Settings**, and use *View public profile* there to see it the way
other people do — which is the only reliable way to check what you are actually
showing.

One account, one profile. There is no per-workspace persona: change your avatar
once and it changes everywhere, including for people in workspaces you barely
use.

## Appearance

**Settings → Appearance** controls how Proyekto looks to you — light and dark,
and the theme colours on top of that. It is a personal setting stored against
your account, so it is yours alone; nobody else in your workspace sees the
consequences of your taste.

## Account-level versus workspace-level

This is the distinction worth internalising, because it explains a lot of
"where do I change this?" confusion:

> Your identity and your preferences follow you everywhere. Your membership,
> your project access and your plan belong to a workspace.

| Belongs to you | Belongs to a workspace |
| --- | --- |
| Name, avatar, profile | Membership, and the seat it occupies |
| Sign-in method and password | The plan and what it includes |
| Appearance | Projects, teams and roadmaps |
| Notification preferences | Who has access to which project |

So: leaving a workspace does not delete your account, and changing your avatar
does not touch anybody's billing. Equally, being a member of a workspace grants
you nothing inside its projects — project access is a separate, per-person
grant. See [Core concepts](/docs/start-here/core-concepts) for why those two
were kept apart, and [Members and seats](/docs/workspaces-and-plans/members-and-seats)
for how membership actually works.

Notification preferences are account-level too, which surprises people: turn
mention emails off and they are off across every workspace, not just the noisy
one. [Notifications](/docs/account-and-apps/notifications) covers the controls.

## If your organisation uses SAML SSO

On Enterprise, a workspace can be wired to an identity provider. Where that is
in place, your account may be **created and deactivated by your IT department
rather than by an invite from a colleague** — you sign in through your provider,
and when your provider deprovisions you, your access goes with it. If that is
your situation, password and sign-in questions belong to whoever runs your
identity provider, not to a Proyekto workspace owner. See
[Plans](/docs/workspaces-and-plans/plans) for which tier includes it.

## Signing out

Sign out from the user menu in the top-right. It ends the session on that
device only — signing out on your laptop leaves the phone app signed in, which
is usually what you want and occasionally not. On a shared machine, sign out
rather than just closing the tab.
