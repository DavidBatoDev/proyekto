You can delete your Proyekto account yourself, at any time, from inside the Proyekto app on Android and iOS or from Proyekto on the web. This page explains exactly what happens when you do, because it cannot be undone and you should know before you start.

## Where it is

Open **Settings → Delete account**. It is in the same place on every platform: in the mobile app, tap your avatar, then Settings, then Delete account at the bottom of the page. On the web it is at the same path.

If you cannot reach that screen for any reason — you have lost access to the account, or the app will not open — email **support@proyekto.tech** from the address on the account and we will do it for you.

## What happens the moment you confirm

Deletion is **immediate and permanent**. There is no waiting period, no "pending deletion" state, and no way to restore an account afterwards. You are signed out on every device.

Signing up again later with the same email address gives you a brand-new, empty account. Nothing from the old one comes back.

Because of that, the flow asks you for two things before it will run: the phrase `delete my account`, typed out, and either your password or a code we email you. Accounts that sign in with Google have no password for us to check, which is why those get a code instead.

## Workspaces and teams you are the only owner of

If you are the only owner of a workspace or a team that other people are still using, it does not disappear underneath them. The flow lists each one and asks you to choose:

- **Hand it to one of its members.** You pick who from a list of the people already in it. Whoever you choose gets full ownership — its members, its projects and anything attached to it. They are not asked first and cannot refuse, so choose deliberately.
- **Delete it, with everything inside.** Its projects, roadmaps, tasks, chat and files go, and its other members lose access.

Some cannot be deleted, only handed on — a workspace with a plan attached to it, or a team with payout records that have to be kept. The screen says so on the row.

A workspace or team that **nobody else is in** needs no decision. It is deleted with your account.

If you only want to leave a workspace rather than delete your whole account, ask one of its owners to remove you instead — see [Members and seats](/docs/workspaces-and-plans/members-and-seats).

## Projects you own

A project you own that **other people have access to** is handed to someone who already works on it, automatically. You are not asked project by project, and nobody gains access they did not already have.

A project that **nobody else has access to** is private by definition, so it is deleted with your account.

## What stays, as a deleted user

Work in a shared project belongs to the team, not only to you. The following stays exactly where it is, with your name on it replaced by "Deleted user" — your email address and your profile do not stay with it:

- messages you sent in project chats;
- comments on tasks, epics and features;
- decisions, deliverables, change requests and risks you recorded;
- your entries in project activity history;
- files you added to someone else's project, task or conversation.

A team's record of how something was decided should not grow holes because one person left. Nothing in that list identifies you afterwards.

Separately, contracts, invoices and payout records that name you are kept for as long as tax and accounting rules require, and then deleted. The [Privacy Policy](/privacy) gives the exact period.

## What is deleted outright

Your profile and everything on it, your email address, your sign-in — password or Google link — your notification preferences, your push notification devices, any API tokens you created, any identity document you uploaded, your AI threads and unsent drafts, and any roadmap not attached to a project.

## If something goes wrong part-way

The deletion runs as a single operation: either all of it happens or none of it does. If it fails, nothing has been deleted and the screen says so, and you can try again safely.

If your connection drops while it is running, the screen offers a **Check status** button rather than retrying by itself. It asks the server what actually happened, because retrying something irreversible on a guess is how you delete something twice.
